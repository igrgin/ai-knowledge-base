Finding 1:

- Severity: Major.
- Dimension: Long-Term Tech Debt & Maintainability.
- Tech Debt Description: T3 now supplies permissions, meaningful-activity classification, accepted-operation authority and audit hooks to nearly every future slice. A single identity implementation that imports project/chat/job/sharing internals would become a dependency hub and force unrelated feature changes through T3. The brief’s “Other review findings” and the application’s feature checkpoints already recognize the required boundaries, but these are still conceptual obligations, not usable interfaces.
- Maintainability Requirement: Keep the dependency direction explicit: resource slices consume trusted account identity and current-permission checks, retain their own ownership/admission rules, and emit bounded audit metadata through an agreed interface. Preserve atomic administrative audit behavior without requiring identity to inspect future domain tables. T3 must not create a second job engine, distribution pipeline, generalized policy language or universal domain-event subsystem. Reuse the existing error/correlation and PostgreSQL test boundaries; select framework session integration before adding duplicate session tracking.

Finding 2:

- Severity: Major.
- Dimension: Long-Term Tech Debt & Maintainability.
- Tech Debt Description: Preserved Google sessions plus automatic development identity introduce multiple independent states: stored Google authentication, effective actor, development provisioning, ordinary permission assignments and normal administrator bootstrap. The intake explicitly leaves non-development disabled-mode identity unresolved. Scattering profile/boolean checks through controllers or overwriting one stored principal with another would make mode changes, expiry and revocation brittle.
- Maintainability Requirement: Resolve this as one bounded mode-to-effective-principal contract in the T3 design. Preserve the accepted Google-session continuity, exclude development provisioning whenever `prod` is active, keep dormant Google deadlines running, and separate development provisioning from normal bootstrap/manager availability. Explicitly settle behavior for requests without valid sessions in non-development disabled mode. This does not justify silently forbidding production disablement or selecting a production development-admin fallback.

T3 belongs around automatic Google account resolution, persistent application sessions and deadlines, development identity isolation, user/admin session controls, permission assignment/enforcement, initial administration and its invariant, audit storage/configuration/inspection, and the consumer-facing metadata/error contracts. Exact default/exception permission storage remains a proposal. Framework/schema/module choices remain design work.

Later slices retain projects and resource ownership implementation; upload staging and durable jobs; private PDF/chat workflows; sharing invitations, general-link acceptance and complete-copy mapping; feature audit hooks; app-wide banner/history/permission presentation integration; and integrated restore/multi-instance evidence. T17 remains withdrawn. T16/T19 sharing decisions are accepted product context, not T3 implementation authority.
